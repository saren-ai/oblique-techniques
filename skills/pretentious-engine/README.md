# pretentious-engine

**Barthes · Mythologies · 1957** · *Structuralist*

*Your grocery list is now a site of contested meaning. You're welcome.*

</div>

---

Take one (1) trivial object.¹
Apply theory until it confesses.²

In 1957 Roland Barthes read soap powder, steak-frites, the new Citroën,
and professional wrestling with the full apparatus of semiotics —
and meant every word. That is the bar.
One heavy lens per reading. Total commitment. Never a wink.

The wink is what separates this from parody, and parody is cheaper,
easier, and not the assignment. The engine guarantees two things
in every reading: at least one term you'll have to look up,
and at least one sentence that is genuinely, uncomfortably true.³

---

<sub>¹ The object is never trivial. That is finding number one.</sub>
<sub>² It always confesses.</sub>
<sub>³ Usually the same sentence.</sub>

## Say this

`pretentious engine` · `theorize this` · `what would Barthes say` · `run it through theory`

Feed it a receipt, a Slack thread, the office microwave sign.
The more mundane the input, the harder the engine pulls.
